# Rarity calibration v2 (player complaint: scores don't match what people would say)

Example: "Ella Langley" scored Deep Cut (85) for a country-singer prompt, but she's had big recent hits ("You Look Like You Love Me", 2024-25) — for this audience she is Schooler (30) at most.

Audience: American teens and young adults in late 2026. Score = how likely THEY are to SAY the answer to THAT prompt:
- 10 Plankton: the first 1-5 things almost everyone blurts out.
- 30 Schooler: well-known; many would say it. Includes CURRENT stars, hits, products, games, memes, shows of 2023-2026 that are popular with this audience.
- 60 Rare: valid and recognizable, but few would think of it.
- 85 Deep Cut: obscure for this audience (older/niche/regional/foreign/technical).
- 100: exactly one, the single least-likely valid answer.
Recency matters: big recent names are LOW (common); old or niche names are HIGH. Fame among this audience beats importance. Check both directions — raise answers that are too common to be rare, lower answers that are too obscure to be common.
